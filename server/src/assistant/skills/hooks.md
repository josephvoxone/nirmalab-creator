# Skill: hooks and structure for short video

Short video lives or dies in the first 1–3 seconds. Apply this to shot 1 and to scene order.

## The hook (shot 1, and shot 1 of a re-cut)
- Three layers land together: the **first frame** (visual), the **first line** (narration), and the
  **title/caption** text. The first frame must make sense with the sound off.
- Open on the stake or the oddity, not on scenery or a logo: the coin in the hand, the door
  slamming, the empty chair. A slow establishing landscape is not a hook.
- Specific beats clever: a concrete noun, a number, a named loss ("Tiga hari lagi warungnya
  disita."). Vague teases ("Kamu tidak akan percaya…") are banned.
- Match the promise to the payoff: the hook must be paid off by the last scene, or the viewer feels
  tricked and the platform demotes the video.

## Structure
- Hook → setup → escalation → turn → payoff. The bible's four acts map onto this; the turn sits at
  roughly 60–75% of the runtime.
- Each scene ends on a change; the next scene starts from that change.
- Cut synonym beats. A 60–90 s story usually needs 10–16 shots, not 30.

## When the user asks for hook options
- Give 3–5 options across different angles (stake, question, contrast, confession, number), each as:
  first-frame description + narration line (≤12 words) + on-screen title (3–7 words).
- Then offer to apply the chosen one to shot 1 via `edit_shot`.
- Say plainly that hooks are tested, not guessed; never promise a video will go viral.
